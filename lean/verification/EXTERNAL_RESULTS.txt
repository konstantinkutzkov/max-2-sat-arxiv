Published results and verification policy
=======================================

Policy agreed with the author, 23 October 2026
---------------------------------------------
Formally verify our contributions and our use of established results. Do not
undertake to formalize the proofs of every established theorem cited by the
paper. Check the exact published statements, assumptions, conventions and
constants carefully, and record any specialization or rescaling. An unfinished
lemma specific to our paper must not be relabelled as a published result.

ExternalResults.lean defines two propositions. It does NOT assert them, add
axioms, or provide proofs of them. The application theorems accept these
propositions as explicit arguments. Kernel checking proves the deductions
conditional on those arguments. Consequently, a clean standard-axiom audit
alone is not a certificate that the external mathematical results have been
formalized. The separate external-results.json register records this boundary.
Source-to-Lean translation and citation review still require human review.

1. Bandeira--van Handel: Rademacher matrix expectation
-----------------------------------------------------
A. S. Bandeira and R. van Handel, Sharp nonasymptotic bounds on the norm of
random matrices with independent entries, Annals of Probability 44(4),
2479--2506 (2016), DOI 10.1214/15-AOP1025.
Published electronic reprint: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.6185
Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2, and the paragraph immediately following Corollary
3.2 (reprint pp. 11--13; journal pp. 2489--2491).

Theorem 3.1 is initially Gaussian. Corollary 3.2 permits independent symmetric
entries whose even moments are bounded by Gaussian moments. The next paragraph
explicitly states that independent Rademacher signs satisfy this requirement.
We use ONLY that Rademacher specialization, with deterministic coefficients.
We do not assume this theorem for arbitrary centered, nonsymmetric entries.

For m-by-n fixed coefficients A and independent fair signs, the bound is
  E ||sign*A|| <= (1+theta)*(sigma_row+sigma_col
                    + 5*b*sqrt(log(min(m,n)))/sqrt(log(1+theta))),
where 0<theta<=1/2, b=max|A_ij|, and the two sigma values are maximum
Euclidean row/column lengths. All matrix norms are Euclidean operator norms.
The formal proposition uses m=n>0, and arbitrary nonnegative upper bounds on
the three maxima. Monotonicity makes this equivalent to the maxima version.
Zero coefficients are allowed. Unused rows in the application are padded with
zeros, so this square version suffices and uses log n.

Important norm convention: signedMatrix is a named Matrix-valued definition.
This avoids Lean inferring its default function-space maximum norm for an
inline two-argument function. The examples check the definitional connection
to Matrix.toEuclideanCLM and hence to the Euclidean operator norm.

Our application, proved in Lean:
- each original independent coordinate is an unordered variable pair;
- upperMatrix places it in at most one array entry;
- Jensen's inequality symmetrizes using an independent copy;
- swapping the two copies coordinate by coordinate preserves the product law;
- this proves independent sign insertion, not merely scalar sign symmetry;
- restriction of a full array of independent signs to the used pair positions
  has exactly the required product law;
- scalar concentration bounds incident squared mass at every vertex;
- incident mass bounds both row and column maxima, including truncated rows;
- second-moment and numerical estimates yield E||R_X||<=2.89*sqrt(n*chi*nu).

2. Ledoux: convex upper tail around the expectation
--------------------------------------------------
M. Ledoux, Concentration of measure and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities,
Seminaire de Probabilites XXXIII, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1709,
120--216, Springer (1999).
Published text: https://www.numdam.org/article/SPS_1999__33__120_0.pdf
Corollary 3.3, p. 165, and its interval rescaling, p. 166.

For a separately convex function with Euclidean Lipschitz constant at most
one and a product measure on [a,b]^d, the displayed upper tail is
  Pr(f > E f + r) <= exp(-r^2/(2*(b-a)^2)),  r>0.
Global convexity suffices, and is the stronger hypothesis used in Lean.
Taking [a,b]=[-B,B], B>0, gives exp(-r^2/(8*B^2)). Strict > is sufficient
for our good event; the cited bound is at least as strong at the endpoint.
There is no assumption of identical coordinate laws, mean zero, or symmetry
in this concentration result. Bounds are needed only on positive-mass outcomes
in our finite-law version. Measurability and integrability are automatic for
finite laws; convex Lipschitz functions are continuous. Empty coordinate sets
have a constant statistic, so their upper-tail event is empty.

The earlier paper proof used a median-to-mean consequence of Talagrand. The
new proof uses this directly published mean-centered inequality instead. It
is stronger and simpler, and preserves the proposition's constants and input
class. No theorem statement or algorithm is weakened or changed.

Our application, proved in Lean:
- operator norm is convex in the original independent coordinates;
- it is 1-Lipschitz: each coordinate appears at most once, the operator norm
  is bounded by the Frobenius norm, and the triangle inequality applies;
- with r=.01*sqrt(n*chi*nu), the norm is at most 2.90*sqrt(n*chi*nu), except
  with probability exp(-n*chi*nu/(80000*B^2));
- under n>=2 and n*chi*nu>=10^9*B^2*log n, this probability is <=n^-3;
- empirical centering is handled by ||R_(X-delta)||<=||R_X||+n*|delta|.
  We never apply either external result to dependent empirically centered
  entries. The supplied-mean case takes delta=0.
