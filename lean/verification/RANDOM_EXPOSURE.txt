Random-input verification: the complete exposure lemma
====================================================

This milestone formalizes lem:random_exposure and the gain definitions
immediately preceding it. It does not verify the concentration estimates
or the exponential running-time guarantee for random inputs. No algorithm,
branching rule, theorem assumption, or claimed exponent has been changed.

THE AUXILIARY EXPERIMENT

The fixed edge set E represents the observed support, with each edge stored
once in increasing original-label order. Its base patterns K_e have the
independent PairLaw laws. ExposureBit E has a separate Boolean coordinate
for every assignment bit and every endpoint of every edge. The whole bit
vector has the independent fair product law and is independent of K.

exposure_joint_law proves equality of full joint laws: multiplying each
base literal sign by its auxiliary endpoint sign produces the original
pair-pattern product law, independently of the uniform assignment. This
works for patterns with stabilizing symmetries, including antipodal pairs.
The correspondence does not assume that different flips yield different
patterns. The already checked observed-support conditioning theorem supplies
the retained pair laws. retained_base_present proves that positive-mass
base outcomes under those retained laws have every edge nonempty.
base_pattern_bound obtains the clause bound from the actual law's support.

GAINS, AVERAGES, AND THE ALGORITHM

gain F i x is the real cast of the integer score difference obtained by
setting x_i to true versus false, holding every other value fixed.
generated_gain proves the exact sum of the fixed unit bias and pairGain
contributions. Multiple clauses per pair and repeated fixed units are
allowed. generate is the actual Formula representation used by the algorithm.

delta F free x i is half the effective literal gap of the residual formula
after fixing variables outside free. generated_delta proves the paper's
earlier/later contribution formula. suffix_average_gain additionally proves
that it equals the expected full score difference under uniform assignments
conditioned on the earlier values. The comparison does not depend on x_i;
including x_i among the averaged unassigned bits therefore does not alter
the paper's average over later values.

delta_preprocess proves this residual quantity is preserved by preprocessing
on the original formula, even when further assignments are then substituted.
delta_core proves that optimizing unit-only variables before preprocessing
does not change the gap at an active variable. algorithm_exposure connects
the exposed signed sum to Random.branchLiteral after exactly these two
initialization steps. Its preferred value is true iff that sum is nonnegative,
including the paper's tie convention. The proof does not postulate a
comparison oracle or replace the implemented branching preference.

WHAT EACH HISTORY REVEALS

knownBefore E free contains the assignment bits and endpoint signs of all
variables outside free. All base patterns are already revealed. Before
processing an unassigned i, current i indexes exactly one distinct fresh
endpoint bit for every incident edge. knownAfterSigns adds this row of
endpoint bits but not the assignment bit of i. list_history_step proves
that revealing that last bit gives precisely the history for the remaining
tail of the algorithm's fixed list. Original labels can have gaps: isolated
variables need not be relabeled or supplied as extra algorithm input.

condition_exposure_history gives the exact conditional law of the whole
experiment after observing K and any specified earlier bit values. It is
the point law at K times the conditioned fair-bit law. Every such event has
positive probability when K has positive probability; no division by a
zero-probability history is used. After conditioning, the new endpoint row
has its full fair product law (current_signs_law). The assignment bit is
still fair even after the row is known (assignment_after_signs_law).

coefficient is the paper's a_tv: an earlier neighbor contributes its base
signed sum over false partner literals, while an unassigned neighbor
contributes half its base signed count. coefficient_known proves equality
on every history event, so these coefficients really use only previously
revealed information. delta_exposure proves the exact representation

    Delta_i = b_i + sum_e rho_(i,e) * a_(i,e).

delta_known_after_signs shows that the row makes Delta_i determined before
the assignment bit is revealed. Together with its fair conditional law,
this proves the asserted independence of that assignment bit from Delta_i.

CONDITIONAL MOMENTS AND THE OBSERVED DEGREE

signed_sum_mean and signed_sum_second prove the finite-product identities
for a weighted sum of independent fair signs. conditional_exposure_moments
applies them to the full experiment conditioned on K and the earlier history:

    E[Delta_i | history] = b_i,
    E[(Delta_i-b_i)^2 | history] = sum_e a_(i,e)^2 = Y_i.

The individual coefficient bound follows from the actual size of its base
pattern. observed_degree proves that the number of incident edges equals
the cardinality of Random.neighbors on the generated formula when all edges
are present. exposure_variance_bound consequently gives Y_i <= m^2*d_i
with precisely the degree extracted by the algorithm, rather than a newly
assumed upper bound or the degree of a different graph.

LATER DEVIATIONS

edge_deviation_exposure proves the paper's identity with eta and the later
theta sign, including both choices of endpoint. eta is a function only of
the base pattern, hence is known initially. deviation_coefficient_bound
proves |eta| <= m/2 from the pattern size bound.

current_theta_law proves that the entire row rho_(i,e)*(2*x_i-1) has the
independent fair law conditional on the preceding history. The common bit
x_i multiplies every sign in the row; joint law preservation, rather than
individual fairness or pairwise independence, justifies this step. opposite_current identifies the deviation coordinate with the current row
at the opposite endpoint. theta_subrow_law proves that selecting the
coordinates belonging to earlier neighbors preserves the full independent
fair law, giving precisely the later-deviation signs in the lemma. No independence of the pruning constraints is assumed.

FILES, STATUS, AND TRUST

FormulaGains and GainAverage connect scores, residuals, and averages.
ProductTools, BlockLaws, and SignMoments provide the finite-law facts.
ExposureGraph defines coefficients and bit coordinates; ExposureSupport
checks the history transition and observed degree. ExposureLaw checks the
full sampling experiment, SequentialExposure checks its conditional facts,
and Exposure assembles the statements and the algorithm connection.

paper-correspondence.json marks lem:random_exposure checked, with the above
declarations and explicit scope. thm:random_main remains partial. Subsequent
work completes lem:random_moments and the finite-space lem:random_ranges;
see RANDOM_MOMENTS.txt and RANDOM_CONCENTRATION.txt. The requirement and
compensation estimates, joint feasibility, and fixed-support candidate bounds
are now checked in RANDOM_ACCUMULATION.txt. Executable-node and observed-support
runtime assembly remain obligations.
Expected and high-probability running times must be established separately.
Graph-family and SBM results retain their prior unverified status.

The build and transitive axiom audit reject proof placeholders, custom
axioms, and native-evaluation proofs. Only propext, Classical.choice, and
Quot.sound are allowed. The probability examples also instantiate a row
with both an earlier antipodal-pair neighbor and a later single-clause
neighbor. These regressions supplement the arbitrary-size proofs.

The correspondence is a mathematical translation reviewed during this
development, not a kernel-certified equivalence between prose and Lean.
The source hashes and statement fingerprints track that translation; they
do not replace independent third-party review.
