Random-input moment estimates: scope and correspondence
======================================================

Completed paper result
----------------------
lem:random_moments (Absolute gains and second moments).

1. Independent fair signs are the existing finite product law fairBits.
   The coefficients a_i are arbitrary real numbers, on any finite index
   type. FourthMoment.signed_sum_fourth proves the exact identity

     E Z^4 = 3 (sum a_i^2)^2 - 2 sum a_i^4,

   equivalent to the paper's diagonal-plus-pair expansion. Its proof
   averages a fresh coordinate and inducts on a finite set. The existing
   signed_sum_second supplies E Z^2 = sum a_i^2. Empty index sets and zero
   coefficients are included.

2. MomentBounds.moment_interpolation proves, for V >= 0 with finite fourth
   moment on a finite measure space,

     (E V^2)^3 <= (E V)^2 E V^4.

   It uses Cauchy--Schwarz for sqrt(V), V sqrt(V), then for V, V^2.
   All integrability requirements are derived from the finite fourth
   moment. The zero-second-moment case is handled separately. The paper's
   proof was rewritten to use these same two inequalities; its statement,
   constants, and algorithm are unchanged.

3. signed_sum_absolute gives E|Z| >= sqrt(Y/3). truncation_loss bounds
   the amount removed by a cap K > 0. signed_sum_symmetric proves the
   whole signed sum is invariant in law under negation, for an arbitrary
   test function. shifted_truncation supplies the pointwise inequality.
   truncated_absolute_gain combines these facts to give precisely

     E min(|b+Z|, K) >= (1/2) (sqrt(Y/3) - Y/(4K))

   for every real shift b and every K > 0. No restriction on b or a
   relationship between K and Y is imposed.

4. MomentBounds.integral_sqrt_lower_bound proves the second assertion on
   an arbitrary probability space, not just a finite sample space. Its
   assumptions are almost-everywhere nonnegativity, measurability,
   integrability of U^2, and E U > 0. Its conclusion uses real powers
   3/2 and 1/2 exactly as in the paper. The proof establishes the positive
   denominator before division. FiniteLaw.sqrt_lower_bound is its finite
   specialization.

5. MeasureBridge constructs an actual probability measure from Law.mass.
   integral_eq_expect proves equality with the existing finite expectation.
   Thus the general analytic statements apply to the same finite laws
   already used for input sampling and conditional exposure. No different
   distribution or unproved expectation identity is introduced.

Regression cases and trust
--------------------------
ProbabilityExamples checks a two-sign exact fourth moment, an empty
signed sum, a shifted and truncated single sign, equality in the moment
interpolation with an atom at zero, and an interval bound with an
out-of-range zero-mass outcome. These complement universal proofs.

Audit.lean includes the public declarations above and audits transitive
axioms. The build excludes proof placeholders, custom axioms and native
evaluation proofs. Only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound are
allowed. The moment statement fingerprint remains unchanged. The later
concentration batch explicitly restricts lem:random_ranges to finite
probability spaces and records that separate statement change.
The correspondence record is a mathematical translation guide, not an
independent certificate that the prose and Lean statements agree.
