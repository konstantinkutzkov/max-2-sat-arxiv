Random-input concentration: scope and paper correspondence
==========================================================

Completed paper result: lem:random_ranges
----------------------------------------
The paper now states this lemma on a finite probability space. Every
sampling experiment in the paper has finitely many outcomes for fixed n.
This explicit scope restriction changes no main theorem, input model,
algorithm, exponent, or constant. It is not a verification claim about
arbitrary infinite filtrations. The general-space moment result from the
previous milestone remains unchanged.

HistoryAverage defines a history as a map H from outcomes to a finite
set. Its conditional law is the original law restricted and renormalized
to H = h. Zero-probability classes receive an arbitrary law; every claim
about their contents is restricted to positive probability, and their
contribution to total expectations is zero. expect_conditional proves
the law of total expectation. mean is the exact conditional expectation.

AdaptiveRanges.Exposure records refining histories, increments known
after each step, conditional centering, and intervals known before each
step. Bounds are needed only on positive-mass outcomes. The public theorem
conditional_ranges subtracts conditional means itself; centering is not
left as a new assumption about the original X_j. It proves precisely

  P(sum_j (X_j - E[X_j | H_j]) < -a) <= exp(-2 a^2 / B)

from a > 0, B > 0 and a deterministic pathwise squared-width budget B.
Lean uses zero-based steps: H_j is known before X_j, corresponding to
H_{j-1} in the paper's one-based notation. The key exponential_budget
lemma retains the random budget inside the exponential. It does not
replace adaptive widths by separate worst-case bounds. No independence
of successive increments or of the pruning constraints is assumed.

BoundedDifferences proves the independent-input corollary for arbitrary
finite coordinate laws, not only fair bits. Each coordinate has a law on
a common finite alphabet; different possible coordinate values may be
encoded in a common finite set. The proof inducts on the number of inputs,
averaging the unrevealed coordinates. The exact 1/8 MGF constant and
exp(-2 a^2 / sum c_j^2) tail are preserved. If all c_j = 0, the statistic
is proved constant and the deviation probability is exactly zero.

BitHistory connects the general history-class conditional laws to the
existing fair-bit history laws and to the full base-pattern/endpoint-bit
exposure experiment. This is an equality of conditional probability laws,
not merely an informal identification of notation.

Local components of lem:random_requirements (now completed)
---------------------------------------------
RequirementMean defines truncatedRequirement using the actual delta of
the generated formula's residual comparison. Its nonpreferred event is
assignment_i != decide(0 <= delta), with the same tie rule as the paper.
The fresh assignment bit and the entire fresh sign vector have their
proved joint product law; fair_nonpreferred_expectation contributes the
factor 1/2 even though the preferred bit depends on that vector.

conditional_truncated_requirement proves the lower bound
  (1/4)(sqrt(Y/3) - Y/(4 cap)).
truncated_mean_constant proves this is at least sqrt(Y)/13 when
cap >= sqrt(Y). conditional_requirement_mean includes a nonnegative
weight. truncated_requirement_le_full compares it to the actual full
requirement [nonpreferred] omega (|delta| + 1).

WeightBounds proves the bounds for Random.weight, the implemented
rational 1/(Nat.sqrt(d-1)+1), not a substituted real weight. For d > 0,
  1/(2 sqrt(d)) <= omega <= 1/sqrt(d).
weight_squared_degree proves omega^2 d <= 1, including d = 0.
RequirementDegree applies the earlier observed-degree variance bound to
cap = m0 sqrt(d). normalized_requirement_mean and
normalized_requirement_bounds prove the local conditional drift and
0 <= X <= m0 for the actual degree and weight.

RequirementTail.drift_lower_tail proves the sequential comparison of A
with the sum of lower bounds on the conditional means. two_deviation_bound
and summed_constraint_failure combine exceptional events by inclusion
and a union bound, without independence assumptions.

These local components are now assembled into the complete paper result.
The prefix instantiation, mean estimate, sensitivity budget, and input-law
transfer are proved in the accumulation milestone; see RANDOM_ACCUMULATION.txt.
The new mean argument replaces the variance calculation without changing c1.

Local components of lem:random_compensation (now completed)
---------------------------------------------

times fresh fair signs, with an adaptive pathwise budget. signed_sum_mgf
proves the exponential estimate for a whole independent signed sum.

CompensationRow defines comparisonMultiplier from the actual residual

Regression cases, audit, and translation review
------------------------------------------------
ConcentrationExamples verifies an adaptive three-bit process: the first
bit chooses which of two later signs contributes. Every path spends
squared-width budget 4, whereas separate worst-case widths sum to 8.
Other cases cover a degenerate independent coordinate, zero influences,
the rational degree-five weight, and a data-dependent preferred bit.
These are checked proofs of examples, not random testing or replacements
for the universal theorems.

All public declarations listed in paper-correspondence.json occur in
Audit.lean. The audit rejects nonstandard axioms, proof placeholders,
and native-evaluation proof dependencies. Only propext, Classical.choice,
and Quot.sound are allowed. The build and source/manuscript fingerprints
are recorded in result.json. The concentration milestone made the finite-space scope of
lem:random_ranges explicit and expanded its pathwise exponential proof.
The later accumulation milestone changes proof prose only.

The correspondence record describes the intended mathematical translation.
Lean's kernel verifies the Lean statements; independent human review is
still needed to assess whether they express the intended paper claims.
