1. The actual rational construction
-----------------------------------
RotationScalar proves the circle parameterization c(t)=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2),
s(t)=2t/(1+t^2), exact orthogonality and a Lipschitz estimate. For a symmetric
pivot block (a,b;b,d), its new off-diagonal entry is
  f(t)=b*(c(t)^2-s(t)^2)+(d-a)*c(t)*s(t).
Since f(0)=b and f(1)=-b, the intermediate value theorem gives a zero on
[0,1]. This real zero is used only in the proof. It is not an algorithm
input or an exact-real computation. If |a|,|b|,|d|<=M, then
  |f(t)-f(u)| <= 16*M*|t-u|.

GridRotation implements the finite minimization over t=j/D, 0<=j<=D.
A floor argument proves grid coverage, and the selected rational pivot is
at most 16*M/D in absolute value. PlaneRotation implements the exact
rational matrix and proves its orthogonality and conjugation identities.
PlaneEnergy proves conservation of the squared Frobenius norm and the
exact identity Enew=E-2*b^2+2*f(t)^2 for off-diagonal energy E.

JacobiStep chooses an actual largest off-diagonal entry by finite argmax.
It handles the empty pivot list, preserves symmetry and squared norm, and
proves E<=n^2*b^2 and the resulting progress estimate. JacobiIteration
composes these actual rational rotations, with early stopping and finite
fuel; its proofs establish both orthogonality and the final energy bound.

JacobiParameters makes every numerical parameter explicit from the input:
  M = ceil(sum_ij J_ij^2)+1,
  D = 32*n^5*M,
  energy target = 1/n^8,
  step decrease = 1/n^10,
  fuel = M*n^10.
For n>0 every active step decreases energy by at least 1/n^10. Initial
energy is at most M. Thus the total computedFrame routine reaches its
energy target within the supplied fuel. It uses the identity for n=0.
FrameResidual proves unitary invariance of the Frobenius norm, identifies
the rational off-diagonal energy with the squared real residual, and obtains
  ||J-Q diag(Q^T J Q) Q^T||_F <= 1/n^4.
There is no numerical existence or termination hypothesis left.

2. Polynomial resource bounds: checked and conventional parts
--------------------------------------------------------------
FormulaScale bounds a clause score and score gain and proves
  |J_ij|<=2*m, sum_ij J_ij^2<=4*n^2*m^2,
  M<=4*n^2*m^2+1
for a well-formed formula with m explicitly listed clauses. Consequently
the actual grid denominator is at most 32*n^5*(4*n^2*m^2+1), and the fuel
is at most (4*n^2*m^2+1)*n^10. These are kernel-checked inequalities.

The paper gives the remaining ordinary bit-cost argument: at t=j/D the
rotation has a common denominator D^2+j^2<=2*D^2. An A update multiplies
its common denominator by the square of this integer; a Q update multiplies
its common denominator by the integer. Matrix magnitudes stay bounded by
Frobenius conservation and orthogonality. Bit lengths therefore grow at
most linearly in the number of iterations times log D, plus input length.
Scanning the polynomial grid and performing polynomially many exact
matrix updates takes polynomial time and space.

This is not a compiler-level cost verification. Nor does the construction
claim polynomial dependence on log magnitude for succinctly weighted
arbitrary matrices. The intended input is the paper's explicitly listed
unweighted formula, and accuracy is the required n^-4. The paper replaces
the previous generic numerical sketch with this sufficient construction.
The comparison search and the input-family claims are unchanged.

3. Concrete formula-only discovery and search
---------------------------------------------
ComputedDiscovery defines discoverFormula using computedFrame, the previous
checked rational sorting, and retained Rayleigh coefficients. For n<r it
uses the zero representation; exactness holds in this bounded-size case.
The actual comparison wrapper then computes its spacing and applies its
previously checked fallbacks, projection grids and comparison runs.

Theorem diagonalized_result_correct proves that this fully specified
algorithm returns an attained optimum for every well-formed input. It has
no callback, approximation-quality, probability or input-family hypothesis.
This closes the statement of lem:community_correctness, which concerns
exactness, not running time.

computed_discovery_guarantees derives all four ApproximationGuarantees for
this actual construction, with residual n^-4. The assumptions concern the
original mean-rank/model and noise/size conditions, not numerical output.
Theorem diagonalized_search_probability combines this with the original
finite product law and gives probability at least 1-1/n of
  nodes <= (4*n+3)^r * (2+14*n) * c^n,  c<2,
where c is the previously explicit exponentialBase/roundRate expression
with K=400*chi. The hypotheses include n>=100, r<=n, density
10^9*chi*B^2*log(n)<=n*nu, and the explicit mean and size cutoffs used in
the paper's proof. The algorithm uses F, r and rational g; it does not
receive the hidden mean or variance. Both positive and zero coefficient
mass branches are covered. No numerical callback or quality guarantee is
assumed. The two published probability estimates are registered explicitly
in external-results.json and printed in ExternalAudit.lean.
