Random-input verification: sampling and observed-support conditioning
===================================================================

This development completes Lemma lem:observed_pairs and verifies the sampling
assumptions used to instantiate thm:random_main. The subsequent exposure
milestone completes lem:random_exposure; see RANDOM_EXPOSURE.txt for its
sequential and algorithm correspondence. No random-input exponential time
guarantee is claimed.

FINITE LAWS AND INDEPENDENT DRAWS

FiniteLaw.Law has a nonnegative real mass at each outcome of a finite type,
with total mass one. Expectations and event probabilities are finite sums.
FiniteLaw.product is the product of the coordinate masses. Its normalization
is proved by the finite product-of-sums identity. These are mathematical
probability distributions, not implementations of a pseudorandom generator.

FiniteLaw.condition is defined only with a proof that the conditioning event
has positive probability. condition_product proves equality of the complete
conditional joint law with the product of the individually conditioned laws
for coordinatewise events. This is joint independence, not merely pairwise
independence. Positivity of every coordinate event follows from positivity
of the joint event; it is not an additional hypothesis.

PAIR MULTISETS AND THE ACTUAL GENERATED INPUT

RandomInput.Pattern m records four multiplicities, one for each literal
pair, in Fin(m+1). This is a canonical representation of a multiset, without
an artificial ordering of identical clauses. The ambient finite type also
contains count vectors whose total exceeds m; PairLaw.bounded requires those
vectors to have zero probability. Thus the law is supported on exactly the
kind of bounded multisets allowed by Definition def:invariant.

PairLaw additionally requires invariance under endpoint complementations
and the stated lower bound on both signed-count second moments.
invariant_iff_endpoints proves that using all four complementation masks is
equivalent to the paper's two separate endpoint-complementation assumptions.
No independence between the four counts within one pair is assumed in this
general model. Different pairs are drawn independently by the product law.

Edge n represents an unordered pair once, using increasing endpoint labels.
generate takes a fixed set of these edges, a sampled pattern for each edge,
and any fixed list of units. It creates the actual Formula type used by the
verified algorithm, preserving multiplicity. generate_wellFormed proves
that every produced binary clause has distinct endpoints.

generated_support identifies all ordered pairs appearing in the generated
formula. generated_edge_neighbors connects nonempty pair patterns directly
to Random.neighbors, the support operation used by RandomFormula before
preprocessing. This is a checked semantic connection, not just a similarity
between two graph definitions. Fixed unit clauses never change this support.

CONDITIONING ON THE OBSERVED GRAPH

The event that a sampled graph has observed edge set H says that each pair
in H is nonempty and every other potential pair is empty. The event has
exactly this meaning for the neighbor relation extracted from generate.
condition_observed_input proves that its conditional joint pair law factors
into the coordinatewise conditional laws.

For a retained pair with retention probability p>0, retained constructs a
new PairLaw with law equal to the original law conditioned on nonemptiness.
It proves the same clause bound and both endpoint symmetries. The squared
signed count is zero for an empty pair. conditional_moment consequently gives

    E[Z^2 | nonempty] = E[Z^2] / p.

Since 0<p<=1 and the expectation is nonnegative, the second moment does not
decrease. The lower bound kappa is retained without assuming a new moment
inequality. These results together establish lem:observed_pairs, including
arbitrary potential supports, observed empty graphs, and fixed unit clauses.

THE TWO CONCRETE MODELS

oneClause is the pushforward of the uniform law on the four literal pairs
to their one-clause patterns. The four choices each have mass 1/4. Its
endpoint second moments are exactly 1. Its size and symmetry conditions
are proved. The ambient pattern type uses the common bound m0=4 employed
in the main theorem; each sampled pattern in fact has size one.

independentClauses is the pushforward of four independent Bernoulli(q)
indicators to their included-clause pattern, for 0<=q<=1. Its size is at
most four, its law is invariant under either endpoint complementation,
and each endpoint second moment is exactly 4q(1-q). quarter_moment specializes
this identity to 3/4. The calculation enumerates only the constant sixteen
indicator combinations for one pair, not all generated input formulas.
The separate closed-form binomial count probabilities are not needed here;
the law is defined directly by the four independent Bernoulli trials.

EXPOSURE FOUNDATIONS

randomized_pairs_law proves that drawing independent base patterns and
independent fair endpoint masks, then applying those masks, preserves the
original product law. Patterns with nontrivial symmetry stabilizers are
allowed: there is no assumption that each pattern has four distinct images.

condition_fair_history proves the exact conditional law after revealing any
set of independent fair-bit coordinates. Revealed coordinates become point
masses; unrevealed coordinates retain their fair product law. Every such
history has positive probability. condition_tensor_left separately proves
that conditioning on arbitrary observations of one independent block does
not affect the other block.

joint_multiply proves that multiplying fair endpoint signs by assignment
signs preserves their joint distribution, including independence from the
assignment data. Several endpoint signs may use the same assignment bit.
The proof is an equality of full joint laws; it does not infer joint
independence merely from individual fairness.

pairGain, signedCount, eta, and theta use the paper's single-edge definitions.
endpoint_deviation proves its signed deviation identity. eta_bound gives
|eta|<=|K|/2; pairGain_bound gives the corresponding |K| bound for an earlier
neighbor's contribution. These are exact algebraic identities and bounds.

The subsequent exposure development assembles these facts into the full
experiment, proves its exact joint law, and connects the full gain and
Delta_t to the algorithm's residual comparisons. The conditional variance
and summed Y_t bound are now checked; see RANDOM_EXPOSURE.txt. Subsequent
work checks the moment lemma and the finite-space conditional-range lemma;
see RANDOM_MOMENTS.txt and RANDOM_CONCENTRATION.txt. RANDOM_ACCUMULATION.txt
records the completed requirement/compensation lemmas, joint feasibility,
and fixed-support candidate expectation/tail bounds. The executable-node
and observed-support runtime assembly remain obligations.

CORRESPONDENCE AND TRUST

paper-correspondence.json marks lem:observed_pairs checked,
lem:random_exposure checked, and thm:random_main partial. It records exactly
which declarations support those statuses. Status counts do not certify
semantic equivalence between manuscript prose and formal definitions.
An independent review of that correspondence remains desirable.

verify.py rebuilds the active project, checks probability regressions, and
audits transitive axioms of the named results. Only the standard propext,
Classical.choice, and Quot.sound axioms are allowed. No placeholder, custom
probabilistic axiom, or native-evaluation proof is used. The solver procedures
are unchanged. The original literal-gap/dense-gap verification remains valid.
