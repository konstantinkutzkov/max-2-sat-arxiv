COUNT AND PROJECTION GUESSING: FIXED-ROUND ANALYSIS
=================================================

Scope and correspondence
------------------------
This batch checks 34 supporting theorems for res:counts and its specialization
lem:count_accumulation. It completes the conditioning and exceptional-event
assembly for the ACTUAL summed comparison, conditional on two concentration
estimates that remain unverified. No numbered paper result changes status.
The algorithm, input classes, and stated theorem constants are unchanged.

H_t is the implemented rational comparison expression; N_tw=(H_t).coeff w,
cast to the reals. interactionMatrix explicitly has Matrix type, and every
matrix norm in these modules uses Matrix.Norms.L2Operator. This is the Euclidean
operator norm, not the default norm of a function of two indices. The restriction
and sign-conjugation constructions also explicitly return Matrix values.

The paper indexes prefix rounds 1,...,ell and assigns the suffix to ell+1.
Lean indexes the prefix rounds 0,...,k-1 and the suffix round k; k=ell. The
rounds are nondecreasing in the original variable order (OrderedRounds), and
all variables have round at most k. P=roundPrefix round k is exactly the
prefix. Empty intermediate rounds are permitted. The final passing theorem
requires k>0 and T=|P|>0, as in the nontrivial paper regime.

Actual compensation, not a replacement statistic
-------------------------------------------------
RoundSplit proves

  compensation H P round x = Q_later(x) + sum_{j<k} Q_j(x)

for the compensation already identified with the implemented comparison sum.
The identity does not assume that creators or contributions are independent.
LaterIncoming sums u_t*N_tw only over actual creators in strictly earlier
rounds, where u_t=2*x_t-1. Q_later=-sum_w u_w*LaterIncoming(w)/2.
Q_j contains all same-round interactions from actual creators in block j.
The decomposition is exact, including the suffix contribution and both
orientations of pairs within a block. LaterRound's first block has zero
incoming contribution. RoundExamples includes a three-variable instance
where Q_later=-1 and Q_0=-1 simultaneously, hence total compensation=-2.
It also checks an empty intermediate round.

Later-round concentration: a simpler proof of the same bound
------------------------------------------------------------
LaterRounds proves that the coefficient incoming at w is fixed by the bits
before w, because its creators lie in strictly earlier rounds and their
preferences depend only on rounds before them. The bits can therefore be
exposed individually using the previously checked predictable-sign bound.
There is no need for an additional block-valued martingale theorem.

For each destination block, form the vector z whose coordinates are u_t for
eligible earlier creators and zero elsewhere. Pathwise, sum_t z_t^2<=T.
If ||N||^2<=V, the squared norm of that block's incoming coefficients is at
most V*T. This remains valid when z depends on the assignment. The first
block contributes zero, so at most k blocks contribute, including the suffix.
Consequently the total squared coefficient budget is k*V*T, and

  Pr(Q_later < -b) <= exp(-2*b^2/(k*V*T)).

The manuscript now explains this individual-bit exposure. With V=K*D_N,
this is precisely the bound used there, with no change to its constants.

Conditional current-round laws and norms
----------------------------------------
RoundHistory proves that observing all earlier rounds fixes the entire
preference mask in the current block. Conditional on any such history, the
restriction of x to the current block has the JOINT fair-bit product law.
All histories arising from an assignment have positive probability; this
is proved from the uniform product mass. The conditional expectation and
probability identities then average fixed-mask bounds over these histories.
No independent-creator or independent-round-event assumption is introduced.

BlockNorms proves that restricting N to a principal block, and conjugating
that restriction by preference signs, preserve the squared norm upper bound.
WithinRounds combines these facts with the quadratic-plus-linear expansion:

  Q_j = -beta^T M beta/4 - <M*1,beta>/4,
  M_tw=c_t*c_w*N_tw, c_t=2*p_t-1, beta_t=-c_t*u_t.

For B>0 with |B_j|*V<=B, the actual linear contribution obeys

  Pr(Q_linear,j < -e) <= exp(-8*e^2/B), e>0.

The quadratic term is transferred to a generic uniform tail bound for fair
signs and symmetric zero-diagonal matrices of squared norm at most V. That
generic bound is an EXPLICIT HYPOTHESIS, not a postulate or a checked version
of Hanson--Wright. All its structural premises for the actual current-round
matrix are discharged. GainStructure separately proves that the original
formula gains, exact count adjustments, and arbitrary supplied projection
adjustments have symmetric interactions and zero diagonal. These proofs use
the actual clause scores and the already checked gain representation.

Combined passing theorem
-------------------------
RoundPassing.round_passing_probability assumes M,V,a>0, the norm budget above,
nonnegative comparison allowances of total at most a, and the following two
remaining probability inputs:

  Pr(sum_{t in P} min(|Delta_t|,M) < 6*a) <= qProfile;

  for every prefix block j and every symmetric zero-diagonal matrix W on
  that block with ||W||^2<=V,
  Pr(-beta^T W beta/4 < -a/(8*k)) <= qQuad.

It proves, for the IMPLEMENTED comparisonBudget.Holds event,

  Pr(pass) <= qProfile + exp(-2*a^2/(T*M^2))
              + exp(-2*(a/4)^2/(k*V*T))
              + k*(exp(-8*(a/(8*k))^2/(V*T)) + qQuad).

The creator concentration and allowance subtraction yield S'>=a outside
the first two events. The later contribution is >=-a/4 outside the third.
Within-round union bounds give sum_j Q_j>=-a/4 outside the fourth. Thus the
tested sum is at least a/2>0, so a passing assignment must belong to an
exceptional event. No independence between these events is used.

The paper substitution is a=g*n*sqrt(D_N)/8, V=K*D_N, k=ell. This gives its
later threshold g*n*sqrt(D_N)/32 and its within-round individual threshold
g*n*sqrt(D_N)/(64*ell). The formal theorem uses strict lower-tail events;
the paper's non-strict upper bounds also bound these events. Using V*T in
the linear term uniformly over blocks avoids division by an empty block's
cardinality and is the same relaxation used in the paper's final estimate.
The final substitution into c_* and the full runtime theorem is still work.
