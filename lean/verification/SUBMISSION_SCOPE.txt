1. Coverage and mathematical boundary
-------------------------------------
The correspondence register contains 38 numbered manuscript statements:
24 checked mathematical statements, 12 checked_core complexity statements,
one explicitly unproved research target, and one unformalized corollary
(cor:random_regular), which combines the checked regular-expander corollary
with published spectral-gap theorems for random regular graphs. There are

All four main families have checked algorithm correctness and exponential

Checked_core does not mean a machine-level runtime theorem. Polynomial

estimates remain theorem hypotheses; no internal gap is hidden as an
external result. The proposed biased-subset extension remains unproved.

2. An explicit change to an auxiliary statement
-----------------------------------------------
Proposition prop:pairmembership now explicitly assumes INTEGER interaction
coefficients. Its earlier wording additionally allowed continuously
distributed real coefficients. That additional generalization is not
claimed as verified, and is not part of this submission's proposition.

Every Max 2-SAT and Max Cut input family studied here already has integer
interaction coefficients. Thus this change narrows the auxiliary analytic
statement, but changes no computational input family, algorithm, constant,
density condition, or main theorem. The existing finite-law Lean results
still allow arbitrary real-valued finite-alphabet coefficients. The new
original-space theorem addresses the actual integer input model without
requiring the sample space, probabilities, or means to be finite/rational.
This scope decision is stated in the paper and correspondence record.

3. Arbitrary probability spaces, with exact transport
-----------------------------------------------------
Random/MeasureInput relates finite Law values to ordinary mathlib measures:
- every probability measure on a finite alphabet defines a Law;
- converting it back gives the same measure;
- finite event sums equal the measure of the event;
- product Laws convert to product measures;
- measurable independent coordinates on any probability space have the
  corresponding product Law, with identical events and expectations;
- almost-sure properties hold at every positive-mass encoded value.

Random/FiniteSupport proves that almost-sure boundedness of finitely many
integer observations yields one finite integer interval containing them
almost surely. A measurable coordinate encoding into that interval agrees
with each original coordinate almost surely and preserves independence.
It transports arbitrary events and expectations exactly. No approximation,
limit argument, rounding error, or assumption about rational probabilities
is used. All events determined by the integer coordinates are measurable.
Random/IntegerMoments identifies encoded centered second moments with
ProbabilityTheory.variance of the original real-cast integer observations.

IndependentPairs/MeasureMembership applies these facts to both alternatives
of prop:pairmembership. The statements use the original expectations,
almost-sure centered bounds, standard variances and density/size conditions.
The empirical event uses the ACTUAL empirical mean, including its absolute
value in the allowance. The supplied-mean case uses the fixed entrywise
expectation and supplied coefficient allowance. The probability is measured
on the original space, and is at least 1-1/n. Existing deterministic lemmas
identify g=tau^(3/2)/(12*sqrt(chi)) and the extra count-accumulation size bound.

4. Full discovery statement on the original space
-------------------------------------------------
Community/MeasureNoise transports the already checked joint energy,
cross-round row and symmetric-norm event to the original integer inputs.
Community/MeasureRuntime proves the mean/noise/diagonal decomposition from
the original formula matrix and symmetric mean, then supplies all numerical
and runtime conditions from the single paper density hypothesis.

integer_discovery_family chooses one positive rational g and constants
C,K,c with C,K>0 and 1<=c<2 BEFORE the input size, probability space,
distribution, hidden mean or added unit clauses. Under the original bounds
and density condition it constructs a MEASURABLE event E with
  Pr(E) >= 1-1/n,
and on E the actual formula-only algorithm satisfies
  nodes <= K*p_r(n)*c^n,
  p_r(n) = (2*n^3+4*n+3)^r*(2+14*n).
The small-size case uses E=the whole sample space. In the other case E is
the original binary-coefficient noise event. For fixed binary coefficients,
the deterministic bound holds for every formula with those interactions.
Units can therefore be chosen after the draw and may use extra randomness.
The theorem supplies a measurable witness rather than relying only on an
outer-measure bound for a potentially nonmeasurable choice of units.

The algorithm receives the formula and fixed class parameters only.
No mean, sampling law, probability space, hidden partition, good event or
finite encoding is an algorithm input. These are proof representations.
Unconditional exactness is diagonalized_result_correct. The numerical
routine is the previously checked finite rational Jacobi construction;
there is no callback or unproved approximation-quality premise.

5. Published results and review
--------------------------------
The same two published estimates are used: the Bandeira--van Handel
Rademacher expectation bound and Ledoux's convex upper-tail inequality.
Their exact statements, source locators, specializations, and all dependent
public declarations are recorded in external-results.json. The new finite
alphabet merely instantiates the existing finite-law Ledoux proposition;
no third external theorem or generalized external signature is introduced.
All new probability-space steps are proved using mathlib and project lemmas.

The paper-to-Lean correspondence is reviewed and fingerprinted, not itself
proved by the kernel. An independent mathematical review of the translation
remains distinct from formal proof checking. No automatic audit establishes
that the natural-language statements express exactly the intended problem.

6. Reproducibility
-------------------
The final audit covers 1,110 public mathematical declarations and fifteen
example modules. Run python3 verify.py with the pinned toolchain and mathlib
revision. The script builds the artifact, checks proof dependencies, audits
the two external-result signatures, checks package revisions, validates all
statement labels/fingerprints, and records source/manuscript hashes. Only
the standard propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound are permitted in the
proof dependency audit. See result.json and RESULTS.txt for the actual run.
