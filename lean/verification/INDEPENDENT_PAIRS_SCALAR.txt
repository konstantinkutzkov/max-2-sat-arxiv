=================================

Historical scalar milestone; its checked results remain valid. The subsequent
matrix application and current external-result policy are documented in

Paper target: prop:pairmembership. Status: partial.
This batch verifies the scalar concentration and empirical-centering stage.
It does not verify the complete proposition or a new runtime theorem.

What is proved
---------------
For independent centered finite-law variables Z_i, |Z_i|<=B on positive-mass
outcomes, and sum E[Z_i^2]<=V>0, the formalization proves

  E exp(t*sum Z_i) <= exp(t^2*V)      when |t|*B<=1.

The proof uses exp(z)<=1+z+z^2 for |z|<=1, averages, and factors the product
law exactly. Exponential Markov with t=a/(2V) gives upper and lower tails
exp(-a^2/(4V)) when a>0 and a*B<=2V, and a two-sided bound twice as large.
Inflating V to V+B*a/2 gives an upper bound exp(-a^2/(4V+2B*a)) for all a>0.
No Bernstein inequality is assumed as a formal hypothesis.

For 0<=Y<=U, Lean proves |Y-EY|<=U and
  E[(Y-EY)^2] <= U*EY.
Taking Y=X^2 and U=B^2 supplies the variance-sensitive bound needed for
squared mass. If r entries have nu<=E[X_i^2]<=chi*nu, the resulting lower
and upper tails have exponents
  -eta^2*r*nu/(4*B^2*chi),  -eta^2*r*chi*nu/(4*B^2).
This avoids replacing the variance budget by r*B^4, which would lose the
sparse regime. The paper includes this elementary argument. Its sufficient
row/column and earlier-row exponents use denominators 8 and 40, respectively,
instead of 5 and 30; the same large C0 and all stated theorem conclusions
remain valid. These final C0 substitutions are not yet formally checked.

The empirical mean of r centered independent entries satisfies
  Pr(|mean|>=a) <= 2*exp(-r*a^2/(4*v))
when each second moment is <=v and a*B<=2v. The same result bounds error
around a common nonzero mean. Bounds are required only on positive-mass
outcomes. Probability masses may be arbitrary real numbers; no uniform-law
or equal-distribution assumption is made.

Matrix correspondence and centering
-------------------------------------
The index Edge n is the subtype of ordered vertex pairs u<v: exactly one
independent coordinate is sampled per unordered pair. pairMatrix reflects
that coordinate into both orientations and has diagonal zero. Lean proves
symmetry, individual entries, the pair count 2*m=n*(n-1), and

  pairEnergy(z) = (sum_{u,v} pairMatrix(z)_{u,v}^2)/n
                = 2*(sum_e z_e^2)/n.

There is no assumption that the two symmetric orientations are independent.
The earlier-row map is injective. Its pushforward law is exactly the product
of the selected original pair laws; earlierEnergy is the corresponding
squared row sum, with t earlier neighbors for the zero-based label t.
This is t-1 for the paper's one-based label.

Subtracting the empirical mean is a deterministic transformation, not a
second independent sampling step. Its exact effect is

  D_centered = D_raw - (n-1)*mean(z)^2.

Lean also proves the row norm inequality

  sqrt(sum (x_i-delta)^2) >= sqrt(sum x_i^2) - |delta|*sqrt(r).

On raw energy between .99*(n-1)*nu and 1.01*(n-1)*chi*nu and mean magnitude
at most .001*sqrt(nu), centered energy is between .98*(n-1)*nu and the same
upper bound. Raw row mass >=.99*r*nu gives centered row norm >=.993*sqrt(r*nu).
All of these are statements about the actual symmetric matrix or its rows.

Simultaneous scalar event
--------------------------
scalar_event_probability works directly under the original product law.
Let m=n*(n-1)/2 and S be any set of positive earlier-row indices. Its
hypotheses are n>=2, B,nu,a>0, chi>=1, centered coordinates, second moments
in [nu,chi*nu], support bounds B, and the explicit inequalities

  a*B <= 2*chi*nu,    a <= .001*sqrt(nu).

It proves that, except with probability at most

  2*exp(-m*a^2/(4*chi*nu))
  + exp(-(.01)^2*m*nu/(4*B^2*chi))
  + exp(-(.01)^2*m*chi*nu/(4*B^2))
  + sum_{t in S} exp(-(.01)^2*t*nu/(4*B^2*chi)),

all three conclusions hold simultaneously:
- |empirical mean|<=a;
- centered total energy lies in the .98 to 1.01 interval above;
- every selected centered row norm is >=.993*sqrt(t*nu).

The proof derives each concentration estimate and uses a union bound.
No independence between the mean, energy, or row events is assumed.
It does not assume the desired probability bound. It has no operator-norm
hypothesis or conclusion. The paper's choice of a=delta_0 and the final
n^-3 and 1-1/n simplifications are subsequent obligations.

Concrete graph inputs and independent copies
---------------------------------------------
bernoulli_scalar_event instantiates the theorem for independent edges with
arbitrary individual probabilities q_e. The actual Max-Cut coefficient is
-2 when an edge is present and 0 otherwise. Its mean is -2*q_e and its
variance is 4*q_e*(1-q_e); both are checked. A stochastic block probability
matrix can supply q_e, but block structure, low rank and discovery are not
used or proved in this scalar instantiation. The positive lower-variance
hypothesis excludes deterministic edge probabilities from the event theorem.
Endpoint examples q=0 and q=1 check the moment identities only.

For an independent copy X', Lean proves the moment identity for X-X', its
bound 2B, and symmetry under sign reversal. For centered X its second
moment is 2*E[X^2]. These are inputs for the remaining matrix estimate;
that estimate itself has not been formalized.
