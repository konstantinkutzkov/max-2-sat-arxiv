Literal-bias milestone: proof guide and scope
============================================

The active algorithm is Max2SAT.Bias.solveBiasPreprocessed in
Bias/Complexity.lean. It implements the fixed-preference prefix/suffix
algorithm now presented in Section sec:bias of dense_max2sat_arxiv.tex.
The superseded adaptive implementation is archived, outside the build.
The numbered-result correspondence is paper-correspondence.json.

INPUT AND PARAMETERS

F0 is the score-preserving preprocessing of the input F. For variable i,
D_i counts binary occurrences, u_i counts unit occurrences, and B_i=D_i+u_i.
The effective signed gap is

  g_i = d2(i)-d2(not i) + 2(d1(i)-d1(not i)).

Assume epsilon, eta in (0,1], R >= 1, and a set S with |S| >= eta*n,
|g_i| >= epsilon*D_i on S. Define d by sum_{i in S} D_i = |S|*d.
Require d >= 1 and sum_i B_i^2 <= R*n*d^2. These are the assumptions of
Theorem thm:bias. The formalization also allows repeated clauses, as long
as a binary clause has two different variables. The node theorem is for
n > 0; the executable solver and its correctness theorem also cover n=0.

THE PAPER'S ALGORITHM

1. Preprocess F, retaining its satisfied-clause offset.
2. Prefer the positive value of i when g_i >= 0, and the negative value
   otherwise. These preferences never change during the search.
3. Divide the variable labels into halves, of sizes floor(n/2) and
   ceil(n/2). Let P be the half with the larger sum of |g_i|. Ties choose
   the second half. This is computable directly from F0; S and the real
   parameters epsilon, eta, R and d are not algorithm inputs.
4. Branch over assignments to P in increasing variable order. At each
   completed prefix, collect the usual strict-improvement cut for each
   variable set against its preferred polarity. Sum these cuts.
5. Enumerate all suffix assignments satisfying this single sum. At each
   suffix node, substitute the partial assignment, cancel complementary
   occurrences, and reject exactly when the remaining bound is negative.
6. Evaluate the completed assignments and retain a best one. The streaming
   implementation keeps no list of outputs or completed prefixes.

The sum can admit assignments violating individual cuts. This relaxation
is intentional and preserves correctness. Adding the individual checks is
not needed for the new bound. The normalization routine is the same
verified occurrence-count cancellation used in the common checker.

WHY AN OPTIMUM SURVIVES

Among all maximum-score assignments, choose one with the most preferred
bits. If a nonpreferred bit could be changed to preferred without reducing
the score, the change would either improve the score or break the tie in
its favor. Both contradict its choice. Thus every relevant strict cut is
satisfied by this common optimum, and so is their sum. This argument
establishes simultaneous compatibility without assuming that arbitrary
redirected assignments satisfy older cuts.

THE CONCENTRATION ARGUMENT

For a full assignment x, let

  L_P(x) = sum_{i in P} [score(x[i := preferred_i])-score(x)].

A preferred bit contributes zero. A nonpreferred bit contributes exactly
the left-hand side minus the bound of its cut, minus one. Therefore any
assignment admitted by the summed cuts has L_P(x) <= 0.

Under a uniformly random full assignment, the mean of the i-th summand is
|g_i|/4. The chosen half carries at least half the total gap, so

  E[L_P] >= epsilon*eta*n*d/8.

Changing bit w changes its own comparison, if present, by at most B_w.
Its effect on every other comparison is at most the number of occurrences
of w in the corresponding cut. Summing over all comparison variables,
that number is exactly D_w. Hence changing w changes L_P by at most 2B_w.
No disjointness or probabilistic independence between neighborhoods is
assumed. This identity handles all their overlaps.

A finite Boolean-cube bounded-differences inequality is proved in Lean,
including its exponential-moment induction and Chernoff argument. With
sum_w (2B_w)^2 <= 4R*n*d^2, it gives

  Pr[L_P <= 0] <= exp(-epsilon^2*eta^2*n/(512R)).

The probability is exactly a count divided by 2^n. In particular, the
number N of complete assignments emitted by the prefix/suffix algorithm
is at most 2^n times this probability.

More generally, with G=sum_i |g_i| and Q=sum_i B_i^2 > 0, the same
argument yields W <= 2^n exp(-G^2/(512Q)). This is exactly the paper's
Lemma lem:bias_count, verified as gap_mass_output_count. The relative-bias
assumptions give G >= epsilon*eta*n*d and Q <= R*n*d^2.

WHY FEW OUTPUTS REALLY GIVE FAST ENUMERATION

For one occurrence-count constraint, complementary cancellation computes
its exact minimum. Thus a passing suffix node always has a satisfying
completion. This property is proved for the executable restriction and
cancellation routines, not assumed as an oracle. The suffix enumerator
emits every satisfying completion once and visits at most 1+2kN nodes,
including rejected nodes, when k variables remain and N outputs exist.

Combining all prefixes gives the actual recursion count

  nodes <= 2^(|P|+1)-1 + 2nN.

Since |P| <= ceil(n/2), the full instantiated bound is

  nodes <= (4+2n) exp((log 2-epsilon^2*eta^2/(512R))*n)
        <= (4+2n) 2^((1-epsilon^2*eta^2/(3000R))*n).

The base 2^(1-epsilon^2*eta^2/(3000R)) is formally proved less than 2.
The finite bound, including the leading polynomial, is more precise than
an O* statement. No assumed concentration estimate, enumeration bound,
cut soundness condition, or solver optimality occurs among its hypotheses.

SPACE, ARITHMETIC, AND THE VERIFICATION BOUNDARY

The following representation bounds are kernel checked:

- The search recursion has at most n+1 levels.
- A pruning cut has at most m occurrences and bound magnitude at most m+1.
- Its accumulated sum has at most nm occurrences and bound magnitude at
  most n(m+1).
- Substitution and cancellation do not increase the occurrence list; the
  normalized restricted bound has magnitude at most the original bound
  magnitude plus twice the original occurrence count.
- The streaming folds equal folds over the mathematical output lists.

These bounds give the ordinary polynomial-space implementation: the
recursion stores partial assignments, polynomial-sized constraints and
one incumbent. Cancellation has at most nm recursive steps, and its
list scans take polynomial work. All stored integer magnitudes are
polynomial in n and m, so their binary encodings have polynomial length.
Every search node takes polynomial time. Preprocessing also performs
only polynomially many scans/reductions. Thus the verified exponential
node bound yields the stated O*(c^n) time and polynomial space in the
usual algorithmic model.

The step from these structural bounds to a bit-operation or physical
memory bound for the compiled Lean executable is a conventional
complexity argument, not a formal compiler or machine-cost verification.
In particular, recursion-depth statements do not claim constant-size
frames. The proof explicitly bounds the sizes of their constraints.
Random-formula, spectral, and SBM results are outside this literal-bias milestone.
The subsequently verified deterministic random-formula correctness is described
separately in RANDOM_CORRECTNESS.txt; its probabilistic runtime remains unverified.
The dense-gap corollary is also checked as described below. The proposed
biased-subset extension in the appendix remains unproved and unverified.

DENSE GAPS ON THE ORIGINAL SIMPLE INPUT

Simple F requires wellFormed F and no duplicates in the list of clause
literal sets. Thus (a or b) and (b or a) are the same logical clause.
The incidence proof counts at most four sign patterns for each other
variable, plus two units: B_i <= 4(n-1)+2 <= 4n.

PreprocessStats proves that no preprocessing rule increases any B_i.
For gaps, the signed mean gain from forcing the positive value is g_i/4.
Equivalent score functions up to an additive constant have identical
such gains, hence identical gaps. The already verified score identity
therefore proves that preprocessing preserves every g_i.

If at least eta*n original variables satisfy |g_i| >= epsilon*n, these
facts give G >= epsilon*eta*n^2 and Q <= 16n^3 in the reduced formula.
For n>0 the positive gap mass implies Q>0. The existing general count
then yields

  W <= 2^n exp(-epsilon^2*eta^2*n/8192).

The same prefix/suffix node accounting gives

  nodes <= (4+2n) exp((log 2-epsilon^2*eta^2/8192)*n)
        <= (4+2n) 2^((1-epsilon^2*eta^2/48000)*n).

The final theorem includes optimality for the original formula and a
strictly sub-2 exponential base. Neither a postpreprocessing bias
hypothesis nor a degree-count bound is left as an assumed premise.
The subset and real parameters are proof witnesses, not solver inputs.
The arithmetic/space verification boundary is unchanged.

TRUST AND REPRODUCTION

Run python3 verify.py in lean/. It builds all modules, checks examples,
audits theorem axioms and pinned dependency revisions, and records
source hashes. Proofs use no placeholders, custom axioms, native_decide,
or external numerical certificates. The only permitted axioms are
Lean's standard propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.
