Random-formula milestone: deterministic correctness
===================================================

This milestone verifies manuscript Lemma lem:exactA. It does not verify
any probability estimate or exponential running-time guarantee for random
inputs. The executable procedure makes no random choices.

FORMULA-ONLY INITIALIZATION

Random.active computes the endpoints of binary clauses in the input.
Random.neighbors deduplicates neighboring variable labels, ignoring signs
and clause multiplicities. Every active variable has positive degree.
The fixed weights are computed before preprocessing:

  weight(i) = 1 / (sqrt_nat(degree(i)-1) + 1).

For positive degree the denominator is exactly ceil(sqrt(degree(i))).
Its positivity and this integer-square characterization are proved.
The order is the active labels sorted by the original input indices.
The stopping depth is ceil(r/2), where r is the number of active labels.
There is no generating graph, planted information, or density parameter
among the algorithm inputs.

Variables outside the observed support occur only in unit clauses.
Random.initial sets each such bit to the value satisfying more units,
with ties choosing false. Random.extend_score proves this optimization
never lowers the original score, while leaving the active values intact.
Residual.restrict constructs the core and its exact satisfied-clause
offset. The already verified Preprocess then acts on that core.
All original active variables remain in the fixed order even when they
disappear from the preprocessed or later residual formulas.

RESIDUAL FORMULAS AND PREFERENCES

A full bit vector together with a list of unassigned variables represents
a partial assignment; only values outside that list are fixed.
Residual.restrict removes satisfied clauses, drops falsified units,
converts binary clauses to units when appropriate, and returns an offset.
Residual.restrict_score proves the exact score identity for every compatible
completion. Well-formedness is preserved.

Random.branchLiteral uses the signed effective gap of the current residual
formula, preferring the positive value on ties. Random.branchCut uses the
paper's strict pruning inequality on that same residual formula. Thus
preferences can and do change with earlier assignments. They are not the
fixed preferences used in the literal-gap algorithm.

For a simple functional representation, the implementation recomputes the
residual from the fixed preprocessed formula at each branching node.
Constraints retain their bounds from creation; the partial assignment is
substituted when testing their sum. This has the paper's substitution
semantics, without requiring a mutable state or a cache. The score-offset
identities justify evaluating the fixed formula during best-solution
selection and the original formula when returning the answer.

RATIONAL WEIGHTED SUMS AND EXACT ENUMERATION

Weighted.Budget stores a rational cost for each value of each variable
and one rational bound. The costs are computed by adding whole-constraint
weights times literal occurrences. Weighted.sum_lhs proves that this is
exactly the weighted inequality in eq:weighted. Weighted.sum_holds proves
that simultaneous satisfaction of the individual constraints implies
satisfaction of their sum for nonnegative weights.

For a partial assignment, assigned variables contribute their fixed cost.
Each unassigned variable contributes the smaller of its two costs to the
minimum. This is complementary cancellation: subtract the smaller cost,
orient toward the cheaper Boolean value, and retain the nonnegative
difference as the coefficient. The formal minimum comes with an explicit
assignment attaining it. Weighted.feasible_iff establishes an exact
feasibility test for this single sum. No joint-feasibility oracle for the
individual constraints is used.

Weighted.enumerate visits exactly the completions satisfying the sum.
Weighted.enumerate_nodup proves there are no duplicate outputs, and
Weighted.nodes_bound gives at most 1+2kN recursion nodes for k unassigned
variables and N outputs. Weighted.fold_eq_foldl connects the streaming
implementation to this list-valued specification. Rational coefficients
are retained as rational numbers, never expanded into literal copies.

EARLIEST-OPTIMUM ARGUMENT

Random.fullTree is the restored decision tree used only in the proof.
At each node its first child uses the preferred value of the current
residual formula. The complete tree covers exactly all compatible
assignments. The full tree and its leaf list are not constructed by the
executable procedure.

ComparisonTree.winner recursively defines its earliest maximum-score leaf.
Random.fullTree_valid proves that a leaf excluded by a new nonpreferred
cut has an at-least-as-good counterpart in the earlier preferred child.
The counterpart need not satisfy constraints from ancestors.

Random.winner_survives proves the key invariant. If this subtree's earliest
optimum satisfies its inherited constraints, it survives the search. On
a preferred child the induction hypothesis applies directly. On a
nonpreferred child, a violation of the new cut would produce an equally
good or better leaf in the earlier child, contradicting the chosen winner.
The inherited constraints and the new cut therefore all hold on the
winner's own path. Their weighted sum cannot reject it. At the stopping
depth the exact suffix enumeration includes that optimum.

The suffix procedure can also emit assignments violating individual cuts;
this is intentional. Evaluating their actual score cannot harm optimality.

EXECUTABLE ALGORITHM AND FINAL THEOREM

Random.fold implements the adaptive prefix recursion and then invokes the
streaming weighted suffix enumerator. Its equivalence to Random.enumerate
is proved, and the shared best-solution update retains a maximum-score
visited assignment. Random.solveOn_correct proves optimality among all
completions of an arbitrary initial partial assignment, for a fixed
nonrepeating variable order, any stopping depth, and nonnegative weights.

Random.solve instantiates everything from the original formula, including
unit-only optimization and preprocessing. Random.randomFormula returns
both the original score and its complete assignment. The theorem
Random.randomFormula_correct proves it returns an optimum for every
well-formed formula, including repeated clauses, empty support, and zero
variables. No unchecked rejection, comparison, coverage, or enumeration
assumption is a premise of that final theorem.

SCOPE AND NEXT OBLIGATIONS

This completes deterministic correctness of the paper's random-formula
algorithm. It does not prove the random-input probability models, exposure
and conditional-independence lemmas, random requirement and compensation
estimates, or the expected/high-probability sub-2^n time guarantees.
The exponential runtime statement thm:random_main is therefore partial.
Subsequent sampling and observed-support conditioning work is documented in
RANDOM_PROBABILITY.txt. The completed exposure, moment, and finite-space
concentration lemmas are documented in RANDOM_EXPOSURE.txt,
RANDOM_MOMENTS.txt, and RANDOM_CONCENTRATION.txt. RANDOM_ACCUMULATION.txt records
the completed accumulation lemmas and fixed-support candidate bounds.
The executable-node and observed-support runtime assembly remains partial.
Formal storage and bit-cost estimates for this weighted procedure also
remain separate work. Existing literal-gap bounds are unaffected.

The appendix biased-subset extension remains an unproved research target.
No declaration or axiom asserting that target has been introduced.
