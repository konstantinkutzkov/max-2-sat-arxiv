Regular-graph Max-Cut: complete mathematical and node-count proof
================================================================

Paper result: cor:maxcut, status checked_core.
Main declarations: Max2SAT.Regular.regular_result_correct,
regular_result_maxcut, regular_nodes_bound, and regular_family_nodes.

Final statement
---------------
For fixed real L >= 1 and 0 < eps <= 1/2, Lean proves that there exist
integer thresholds D,N and real constants c,C with 1 <= c < 2 and C > 0
such that every d-regular simple graph on n vertices satisfying

  d <= (1-eps)*n,
  every adjacency eigenvalue except one occurrence of d has magnitude
    at most L*sqrt(d)

has actual regularNodes <= C*(n+1)*(2+14*n)*c^n. The eigenvalue hypothesis
is vacuous for n<=1, matching the paper's convention lambda(G)=0.
The statement permits an arbitrary list of input unit clauses. The constants
are fixed for the family, independently of n, the graph, and those units.

EigenBound uses mathlib's actual Hermitian eigenvalues, with multiplicity.
It does not assume a mixing estimate, an operator estimate, or an abstract
spectral certificate disconnected from the input graph. Exactly one
occurrence of d is excluded. In the high-degree branch the remaining bound
is strictly smaller than d, so repeated occurrences cannot be hidden.
The low-degree branch does not need the spectral hypothesis.

The algorithm and its score
---------------------------
Regular.formula contains (u or v) and (not u or not v) for every edge,
and the supplied units. formula_coeff identifies the actual score-derived
interactions with -2 times the adjacency matrix. formula_score proves

  score(formula,x) = score(units,x) + number of edges + cutSize(graph,x).

regularResult fixes integer thresholds for the family. If n<N it enumerates
all assignments. Otherwise it computes the input degree from vertex 0. If
this degree is below D, it constructs a greedy independent set, enumerates
assignments to its complement, and optimizes the unit-only residual. In the
remaining case it runs the unchanged preprocessed, two-round count search
with beta=-2*d/(n-1), floor(n/4) first-round vertices and floor(n/2) total
prefix vertices. The ordering is fixed. There are n+1 count guesses.

regular_result_correct proves optimality on every graph, without regularity,
density, or eigenvalue assumptions. regular_result_maxcut specializes to no
units. Only the improved node bound needs the family's hypotheses.

Spectral and combinatorial derivation
-------------------------------------
Rows: the corrected matrix has diagonal zero and off-diagonal entries
  -2*A_uv + 2*d/(n-1).
Every squared row norm is D_N=4*d*(1-d/(n-1)). For sufficiently large n,
  2*eps*d <= D_N <= 4*d, and |beta| <= 2.

Centering and Spectral: let B=A-(d/n)*11^T and P=I-(1/n)*11^T.
Lean proves the projection's energy bound and N=-2*B+beta*P. It derives
||B|| <= L*sqrt(d) from the actual adjacency eigenbasis: the constant vector
lies in the excluded eigenspace, and every zero-sum vector has zero
coordinate there. The squared coordinate sums give the operator bound.
Thus ||N|| <= 2*L*sqrt(d)+2 <= 3*L*sqrt(d) in the high-degree range.

Mixing: Cauchy-Schwarz and the actual centered operator bound give the
edge-count deviation inequality. Rounds checks the exact cardinalities of
the two rounded prefix sets, their disjointness and relation to the count
algorithm. For n>=80 and d>=64*L^2 it proves e(B1,B2)>=d*n/40.
No equality of the two round sizes is assumed.

Profile: each cross-edge contributes at least eps^2 to the squared row
profile W_t. Summing sqrt(W_t) >= eps*degree_B1(t)/sqrt(d) gives
  sum_{t in B2} sqrt(W_t) >= eps*n*sqrt(d)/40.
Rounds identifies this sum with the actual count algorithm's earlier-round
profile, including zero contributions in the first round.

Parameters: the paper's constants g=eps/(320*sqrt(3)) and
K=9*L^2/(2*eps) meet the existing count theorem's norm and profile bounds.
T*|beta|<=2*d; if g^2*eps*n>=128, then
  (16*d)^2 <= g^2*n^2*D_N,
which discharges its comparison allowance. Encoding supplies the bridge
from original clause scores to these matrix coefficients. HighDegree then
applies the fully verified count theorem with no analytic hypotheses left
unproved. Its bound is (n+1)*(2+14*n)*countBase^n, with countBase<2.

Bounded degree and uniform assembly
-----------------------------------
Greedy explicitly chooses vertices and filters their neighbors from a
finite list. It proves independence and coverage, then
  n <= (D+1)*|S|
under maximum degree <=D. IndependentSearch proves that assigning the
complement leaves only unit clauses on S and that its completion is optimal
among all extensions. The streaming fold returns an optimum, and its exact
recursive-call count is 2^(n-|S|+1)-1, including the initial call.
LowDegree bounds this by 2*degreeBase(D)^n, where
  degreeBase(D)=exp(log(2)*(1-1/(D+1))) < 2.

Runtime proves existence of thresholds with
  D >= 64*L^2, N>=80, eps*N>=2, g^2*eps*N>=128.
It sets c=max(degreeBase(D),countBase(L,eps)), proves 1<=c<2, and combines
all branches. C=2^(N+1) suffices, including the finitely many small inputs.
The thresholds are not extra assumptions on the final family theorem.

Scope and remaining work
-------------------------
The formal runtime model counts recursive calls, including rejected calls.
Polynomial graph-to-formula conversion, bit-arithmetic overhead, and streamed
polynomial space remain the ordinary implementation arguments in the paper.
The graph encoding uses Finset.toList, hence a noncomputable list ordering
in Lean. Its score and coefficient proofs are independent of that ordering;
this is a mathematical specification of the input formula, not a claim to
have certified the compiled cost of converting graph representations.

The theorem starts from the paper's eigenvalue hypothesis. The constructions
and cited eigenvalue facts for named Paley and Ramanujan families are not
formalized. Independent-pair and SBM estimates, numerical discovery of
hidden communities, and fallback integration remain outstanding. The
biased-subset appendix is an unproved research proposal.

The correspondence record and fingerprints assist independent review but do
not prove semantic equivalence between the prose and the Lean statements.
Reproduce the full check with python3 verify.py in the artifact directory.
