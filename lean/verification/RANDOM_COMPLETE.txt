Complete arbitrary-support random-sign runtime verification
===========================================================

Scope and final statements
--------------------------
thm:random_main and thm:weighted now have checked_core status. The complete
mathematical probability argument, deterministic correctness, and actual
initialized recursive-call bounds are checked. The algorithm takes only the
formula; neither the generating support nor its distribution is an input.
No probability or candidate-count estimate is assumed in the final theorems.
The paper's algorithm, input families, and constant formulas are unchanged.
The general model now explicitly types its clause-count bound m0 as a positive
integer and kappa as real, matching the finite multiplicity representation.

For a fixed arbitrary potential support E on n original labels, independent
PairLaw m0 kappa draws with m0>0 and 0<kappa<=m0^2, and arbitrary fixed unit
clauses, FullRuntime proves, with c0=runtimeConstant m0 kappa,

  E nodeCount <= (4+6*n)*2^n*exp(-c0*n),
  P(nodeCount > (4+2*n)*2^n*exp(-c0*n/2)) <= 3*exp(-c0*n/2).

All recursive invocations are counted, including immediate rejection.
The two exponential bases are strictly below two because c0>0; the proof
also establishes c0<(log 2)/2 to absorb the rounded prefix cost.
Random.randomFormula_correct and generated_input_exact prove optimality
on every well-formed generated input, not merely with high probability.

ConcreteRuntime instantiates both distributions in thm:random_main:
- exactly one uniformly chosen signed clause per potential pair;
- each of the four signed clauses independently present with probability 1/4.
Both use m0=4 and kappa=3/4, as in the manuscript. The Boolean model flag
selects a probability law in the theorem, not an input to the algorithm.
Empty potential supports, empty observed supports, unit-only variables,
and n=0 are included. There is no minimum-density assumption.

Why no renaming proof is needed
------------------------------
ActiveSupport defines the vertices incident to a support on the original
Fin n labels. Its influence budget charges only these vertices. Inactive
coordinates have zero degree and zero influence. ActiveConcentration then
repeats the previous tail assembly with r=card(activeVertices E), not n.
The inequality 2*T>=r is proved for the actual ceil(r/2) prefix.

ActivePaths checks the increasing order with skipped inactive labels.
Removing the clauses on inactive variables preserves active score gains
when their initialized values are fixed. Removing inactive coordinates from
a free suffix cannot affect any comparison, since every binary endpoint is
active. The actual list comparisons equal the analytic comparisonSum.

ActiveCounting proves the exact count identity for any predicate P depending
only on a set S of coordinates and any fixed values outside S:

  card {x : x agrees with base outside S and P x}
    = 2^card(S) * P_uniform-original-bits(P).

Thus the ambient uniform probability can still use all original n bits,
while the initialized search counts each active assignment once. No extra
factor 2^(n-r) is introduced. This is a proved finite-set bijection and
coordinate-projection law, not an assumed invariance of the search.

ActiveCandidates applies that identity to the initialized algorithm,
including rational weights, unit-only initialization, core restriction,
preprocessing, tie choices, and the strict +1 comparison offset. This
identifies the candidate count exactly even under score equivalence with
a constant offset. ActiveNodes derives the fixed-support expectation with
arbitrary original labels. Early pruning can remove additional candidates;
only output implies full-prefix feasibility is used, not the converse.

From observed supports to the original input law
------------------------------------------------
ObservedRestriction defines selection of nonempty pair draws without
renaming vertices. It proves preservation of scores (up to the stated
offset), observed neighbors, weights, and initialized candidate counts.
The conditional law of the selected patterns is exactly the product of
the original pair laws conditioned to be present. Symmetry, boundedness,
and the second-moment bound for that law were established earlier.

ObservedPartition partitions the original sample space by its observed
edge set and applies the preceding expectation to each positive-probability
fiber. For an empty observed support the active set is empty and the
candidate count is exactly one; the search charges one terminal call.
No conditional event of probability zero is used.

SupportAveraging proves that 2^r*exp(-c0*r) is increasing in r and r<=n.
The finite law of total expectation gives

  E candidateCount <= 3*2^n*exp(-c0*n).

The finite nonnegative Markov inequality gives the corresponding tail.
FullRuntime combines this with the deterministic initialized node bound
and absorbs the prefix cost. ConcreteRuntime supplies both original model
wrappers with the common constants and all-input correctness.

Trust, tests, and remaining scope
--------------------------------
All new public theorems are included in Audit.lean. SupportExamples checks
a count with nonconsecutive active labels, empty observed support despite
an arbitrary potential support, unit-only initialization, and zero variables.
These are kernel proofs; no random testing is used as proof evidence.
At this milestone the build ran twelve example modules. The dependency audit, statement
correspondence, and source/manuscript fingerprints are recorded in result.json.
Only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound are permitted.

checked_core is deliberately narrower than a verified machine-time bound.
Polynomial bit-arithmetic overhead and streamed polynomial space use the
ordinary implementation arguments in the manuscript; no compiler-level
cost semantics is claimed. The regular-graph result is now checked_core; see
REGULAR_GRAPHS.txt for its complete mathematical and node-count proof.
Independent-pair and SBM concentration, hidden-community numerical discovery
and fallback integration remain outstanding. The biased-subset appendix
is an unproved research target, not a formalization obligation with a proof
already available.

The correspondence record identifies the formal statements supporting each
paper result. Its translation still requires mathematical review: neither
successful kernel checking nor matching fingerprints proves equivalence
between the prose theorem and the Lean theorem.
