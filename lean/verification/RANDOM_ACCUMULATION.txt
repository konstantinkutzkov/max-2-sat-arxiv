Complete random-input accumulation estimates
===========================================

Completed paper results
-----------------------
lem:random_requirements and lem:random_compensation are now checked in
full, with the constants stated in the paper. The analysis is on a fixed
observed support with vertices indexed in their fixed branching order.
Lean uses Fin n and zero-based indices; the paper uses 1,...,r. Here n=r.
The charged set C has cardinality T and is the paper's prefix. The proved
bounds allow any fixed C, so the prefix is a specialization, not an extra
formal hypothesis left to verify. Every charged vertex has positive degree.
The paper fixes a nonempty observed graph, so m0>0 and T>0.

OrderedExposure defines the unassigned suffix and its exact history classes.
Its supportWeight equals the actual rational Random.weight on every
nonempty observed edge pattern. Isolated labels are allowed by the local
statements; their accounting in the full executable algorithm is a separate
runtime obligation below.

Accumulated requirements
------------------------
RequirementAccumulation defines requirementAt and requirements from the
actual residual delta, preferred value decide(0 <= delta), and event of
choosing the nonpreferred bit. The strict offset is +1, as in A_T.
It proves adaptation, conditional drift, local range, domination of the
truncated sum by A_T, and the whole-order squared-range budget m0^2*|C|.
requirement_drift_tail_fixed proves the first deviation bound for each
bounded base-pattern outcome; requirement_drift_tail averages it over the
actual base-pattern law, using its proved support bound.

EdgeDataLaw proves that the base patterns together with the two transformed
theta signs on each edge have the independent product law used for bounded
differences. thetaTransform is a probability-preserving involution of the
full bit experiment. The same assignment bit may affect several endpoint
signs without invalidating this joint-law identity. data_coefficient_exposure
and drift_data_exposure identify the edge-data statistic with the actual
available drift. No independence of pruning constraints is assumed.

EdgeVariance proves the lower expectation of each squared coefficient,
then E Y_t >= kappa*d_t/4. The formerly used variance calculation is
unnecessary: 0 <= Y_t <= m0^2*d_t directly gives

  sqrt(Y_t) >= Y_t/(m0*sqrt(d_t)),
  E sqrt(Y_t) >= kappa*sqrt(d_t)/(4*m0).

DriftMean proves this implies the paper's original constant

  c1 = sqrt(kappa)/(2*sqrt(1+4*m0^4/kappa^2)).

Indeed c1 <= kappa/(4*m0); the scalar comparison follows from
(kappa-2*m0^2)^2 >= 0. The paper proof now uses this shorter argument.
No theorem statement, numerical constant, input family, or algorithm changed.
The previously checked general square-root moment lemma remains a valid
paper statement but is no longer needed for this application.

DriftSensitivity proves that changing one edge affects only its endpoints,
each square root changes by at most m0, and the total squared sensitivity is
at most 2*m0^2*n/169. The degree identity and normalization budget are proved,
not assumed. Bounds outside a distribution's support are handled by the
bounded extension clippedPattern: it leaves every allowed pattern unchanged
and sends disallowed patterns to zero. This is only an extension of the
statistic for applying a concentration theorem, not a modification of the
input distribution or algorithm.

DriftTail applies bounded differences and transfers the result back through
the exact edge-data law. AccumulatedRequirements.requirements_tail combines
the two deviations and proves exactly

  P(A_T < c1*T/26-a-b)
    <= exp(-2*a^2/(T*m0^2)) + exp(-169*b^2/(m0^2*n))

for a,b>0. All application hypotheses are discharged.

Compensation
------------
CompensationSum defines compensation by the actual score gains minus their
residual averages. ordered_gain_deviation proves that only later neighbors
contribute. compensation_edges and compensation_rows identify the entire
Q_T with its edge terms and then incoming rows.

CompensationTail proves each row is known after its variable is processed,
its earlier comparison coefficients are known beforehand, and its conditional
exponential estimate has the stated squared-coefficient allowance.
total_compensation_budget charges each edge once to its earlier endpoint
and proves the total is at most m0^2*|C|. The degree-normalization hypothesis
of the generic intermediate bound is discharged by support_weight_squared_degree
in compensation_tail_fixed. compensation_tail averages over all base patterns
under the actual PairLaw product distribution. Its conclusion is exactly

  P(Q_T < -c) <= exp(-2*c^2/(m0^2*T)).

The paper proof now groups fresh signs by their later vertex, as Lean does.
It uses conditional moment-generating bounds, not an incorrect claim that
the entire row has a short deterministic range. No independence between
requirements and compensation is needed.

Additional checked consequences for the main proof
--------------------------------------------------
SumFeasibility.comparison_sum_identity proves the exact identity between the
weighted sum of strict score comparisons and A_T+Q_T. Its sign convention
and +1 offset agree with the paper, including preferred-value ties.
sum_feasibility_tail uses a=b=c=c1*T/104, T>=n/2, and the two completed lemmas
to obtain 3*exp(-c0*n), with c0=c1^2/(10816*m0^2). All scalar exponent
comparisons are checked. sum_feasibility_input_law transfers this event
from the auxiliary exposure to the original independent pair draws and
an independent uniform assignment.

CandidateProbability defines candidateCount as the number of assignments
satisfying that summed comparison at their own prefix. The exact uniform
probability/count identity and finite nonnegative Markov inequality give

  E candidateCount <= 3*2^n*exp(-c0*n),
  P(candidateCount > 2^n*exp(-c0*n/2)) <= 3*exp(-c0*n/2).

These count the mathematical candidates on a fixed support, not the actual
visited nodes of the executable search. Their prefix comparisons use the
full assignment only through the actual residual average at that prefix.

Regression cases and audit
--------------------------

out-of-support pattern, the original constant comparison, a fixed-prefix
candidate bound on a three-vertex path with unit clauses, and a two-variable
Max-Cut encoding at the exact A_T+Q_T=0 feasibility boundary. These examples
supplement the arbitrary-size proofs.

are recorded in result.json. Only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound
