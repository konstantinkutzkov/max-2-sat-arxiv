Actual comparisons and the common gap
-------------------------------------
GapBridge.average_signed_form proves, over the exact rational values used
by the algorithm,

  average(A,S,x) = A.constant + sum_w A.coeff(w)/2
                    + sum_{w in S} A.coeff(w)*direction(x_w)/2.

average_real_signed_form casts this identity to the real-valued probability
analysis. earlierRow is exactly the earlier-round interaction row L_t.
comparison_gap_mean therefore bounds the first absolute moment of the
computed average, not a separately specified random surrogate.

GapProfile.shifted_absolute_mean proves that a fixed shift cannot lower
E|Z| for a symmetric signed sum Z. It uses the joint sign-flip law and the
triangle inequality, and then the previously verified fourth-moment bound.
row_gap_mean supplies the factor 1/2 in E|Delta_t| >= sqrt(W_t/3)/2.
There is no restriction on the constant shift (hence no restriction on units).

For z=(Lu)_t, the two scalar clipping estimates are

  |H_t| <= M/2: min(|H_t+z/2|,M) >= |H_t+z/2|-z^2/(8M),
  |H_t| >  M/2: min(|H_t+z/2|,M) >= M/4-z^2/M.

Their sum gives clipped_sum_lower: proxy - E/M <= clipped gap sum whenever
sum z_t^2 <= E. This improves the paper's 9/8 loss to 1, but no theorem
constant is changed. The paper proof now explains this simpler row-by-row
argument before retaining its original weaker bound. gap_proxy_mean proves
the proxy's expected lower bound from the explicit per-row square-root
condition. It does not assume independent rows: all rows may share bits.

The remaining common-gap obligations are convexity and the Euclidean
Lipschitz estimate, Talagrand concentration, integration for the mean/median
shift, and the full substitution of norm/profile/size hypotheses. The file
contains no assumed Talagrand theorem and no axiom replacing this work.

Creator indicators and allowance subtraction
-------------------------------------------
BitExposure observes the actual assignment bits in increasing label order.
bit_history_fresh proves their fair conditional laws. assignment_sign_tail
instantiates the existing finite martingale theorem with these exact histories
and permits a pathwise total squared-coefficient budget. It does not replace
that total by the sum of separate worst-case coefficients.

OrderedRounds means that an earlier round implies an earlier variable label.
Thus both singleton rounds and consecutive blocks from the paper satisfy it.
average_known and preference_known prove that the implemented averages and
preferences are determined before the current bit. All other earlier bits,
including earlier members of the same round, may also be revealed.

CreatorTail.creator_centering proves

  [x_t != p_t]*w_t - w_t/2 = -sign(p_t)*w_t*sign(x_t)/2.

For w_t=min(|Delta_t|,M), creator_gap_tail proves the exact estimate

  P(sum creators w_t < sum_{t in P} w_t/2 - a)
    <= exp(-2*a^2/(|P|*M^2)).

This is for the algorithm's actual preferences; it does not assume that
creator decisions are independent across steps. M,a and |P| are positive
where they appear in denominators. accumulated_gap_lower handles the
rational allowances, using their nonnegativity. accumulated_gap_of_profile
checks the paper's constants with a=g*n*sqrt(D_N)/8: a clipped profile of
at least 6a, creator deviation at most a, and total slack at most a imply
S' >= a.

Sequential compensation and the norm bridge
------------------------------------------
SequentialCompensation defines the incoming coefficient at w as

  sum_{t in P, t<w, t creator} sign(x_t)*N_tw.

incoming_known proves its dependence only on earlier bits. The exact cast
of the IMPLEMENTED compensation is -1/2 times the sum of these coefficients
multiplied by sign(x_w). The proof requires the zero diagonal, as in the
paper's off-diagonal interaction matrix.

SequentialEnergy expresses the squared Euclidean norm inequality for R^T
on every vector. The random vector used in the proof has coordinate sign(x_t)
at creators and zero elsewhere; its squared length is at most |P|. Its
adaptivity does not prevent application of a deterministic operator bound.

OperatorEnergy uses mathlib's explicit Matrix.Norms.L2Operator instance.
This matters: the default norm on functions or matrices would not express
the Euclidean hypothesis in the paper. sequentialMatrix is the rectangular
matrix indexed by prefix variables and all variables, with coefficient N_tw
when t<w and zero otherwise. The transpose norm equality and mulVec norm
bound prove SequentialEnergy from ||R||^2 <= V. The resulting theorem is

  P(Q < -b) <= exp(-2*b^2/(V*|P|)).

The formal tail is strict. The passing-bound assembly below uses b=a/2 and
S'>=a, so Q<=-a implies the strict event Q<-a/2. Thus equality cases at the
constraint boundary are included without assuming a zero-probability tie.

matrix_squared_energy also supplies generic signed-vector, row-sum, and
Frobenius estimates. With ||N||^2 <= V they give the budgets |B|*V used in
the within-round proof. Specific block restriction and norm-preservation
instantiations are still part of the remaining round assembly.

Actual passing event
--------------------
SequentialPassing.sequential_passing_probability combines the creator and
compensation bounds with comparison_sum_identity and the rational budget.
Its common-profile hypothesis is explicitly

  P(sum clipped gaps < 6a) <= q.

With total slack <= a and the squared operator bound V, it proves

  P(the actual summed budget holds)
    <= q + exp(-2*a^2/(|P|*M^2)) + exp(-a^2/(2*V*|P|)).

No independence between these exceptional events is assumed. This supplies
the passing probability to the already checked node-count conversion once
the common profile bound and class parameters are established. It does NOT
establish that profile bound, so it is not the full runtime theorem.

Within one round
----------------
RoundCompensation verifies the pair identity for all four choices of each
assignment/preference bit and for arbitrary real interactions, then sums it.
For a fixed preference mask p, let c_t=sign(p_t), beta_t=-c_t*sign(x_t), and
M_tw=c_t*c_w*N_tw. For symmetric N it proves exactly

  Q_j = -beta^T M beta/4 - <M*1,beta>/4.

Symmetry and zero diagonal are separately proved to survive conjugation.
changed_sign_law proves the JOINT fair-bit law of beta for each fixed mask.
The linear tail is proved from the checked cosh moment-generating-function
bound: if sum a_t^2<=B, the probability that -sum a_t*beta_t/4<-e is at most
exp(-8*e^2/B). Preferences in the actual algorithm are fixed by earlier rounds;
the subsequent COUNT_ROUNDS.txt batch now verifies their conditional block
instantiation, later-round compensation, and summation over blocks.
The quadratic term's Hanson-Wright bound remains unverified. No custom axiom
or assumed quadratic tail is used to label any result checked.
