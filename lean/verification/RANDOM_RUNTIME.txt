Random-input search accounting and fixed-support runtime
========================================================

Scope
-----
Historical milestone report. RANDOM_COMPLETE.txt supersedes the remaining
obligations listed below: the arbitrary-support theorems are now checked_core.

This batch connects the completed random-input candidate estimates to the
actual initialized RandomFormula search. It formalizes Step 3 of the proof
of thm:random_main, on a fixed nonempty support whose indexed vertices are
all active, and the corresponding step for thm:weighted. It does not yet
complete these two arbitrary-support runtime theorems. Their status remains
PARTIAL. The algorithm, input families, and theorem constants are unchanged.

The deterministic accounting below works for every formula, including
inactive variables. The final probabilistic specialization uses Fin n for
the active vertices in their fixed order: here n is the paper's r. This
distinction is the remaining assembly obligation, not a new restriction
on the paper's claimed input families.

All recursive calls and outputs
-------------------------------
Random.nodes mirrors the existing Random.enumerate/Random.fold recursion.
It charges one for every invocation, including a call that immediately
rejects its assignment. At the stopping depth it uses the previously
verified Weighted.nodes suffix-enumeration count. Random.nodeCount applies
the exact formula-only initialization: active variables, increasing original
labels, original neighbor weights, fixed unit-only values, core restriction,
preprocessing, and stopping depth ceil(r/2). Computing this initialization
has polynomial overhead; it is not included as machine instructions in the
recursive-call count.

SearchNodes proves, for prefix length T and suffix length k,

  nodes <= 2^(T+1)-1 + 2*k*(number of outputs).

The outputs have no duplicates. In particular, the initialized search has

  nodeCount F <= 2^(ceil(r/2)+1)-1 + 2*r*(number of outputs),
  r = card (Random.active F).

An empty active set has one terminal call and one initialized output.
The streaming fold is already proved equivalent to folding its conceptual
output list; the implementation does not materialize that list.

Why the mathematical count is an upper bound
--------------------------------------------
PathBudget defines the sum of comparisons along a complete assignment's
prefix, even if an earlier accumulated sum would have rejected that path.
The residual formula, preferred polarity and created constraint depend only
on values already assigned; agreement on those values is sufficient.
output_path_budget proves that every actual output satisfies its full-prefix
sum. There is no assertion that all assignments satisfying the final sum
are output: early rejection may remove some of them. This implication is
all the counting argument needs.

SearchCounts defines initialized pathCandidates with the inactive coordinates
fixed to their actual initial values. The no-duplicate and path-budget facts
give outputs_le_candidates and nodes_le_candidates for every formula.
expected_nodes_of_candidates is a generic finite-distribution consequence;
its candidate-expectation assumption is discharged in FixedSupportNodes.

Exact connection to score comparisons
--------------------------------------
ScorePaths.branch_cut_excess identifies the real value of the actual rational
constraint's left side minus its bound with

  sign(preferred polarity) * gain(original formula, variable, assignment) + 1.

Residual score offsets cancel. The formula is well formed (binary clauses
use two distinct variables); multiplicities and arbitrary unit lists are
permitted. path_excess_score sums this identity with the actual rational
weights. list_comparison_preprocess proves that preprocessing preserves the
comparison statistic, including the preferred-value tie rule. The strict
offset +1 is present throughout.

CanonicalOrder proves that taking the first ceil(n/2) elements of the
increasing Fin n list gives precisely the ordered suffixes in the analytic
exposure. Its CanonicalPrefix condition is discharged, not assumed of the
input distribution. It also connects full-active initialization to the
original formula. initialized_candidate_identity proves equality between
Random.candidateCount and the analytic RandomInput.candidateCount on this
prefix, using the previously checked identity between observed rational
weights and supportWeight.

Fixed-support expectation and tail
----------------------------------
Let E be a fixed support on n>0 vertices, every vertex incident to an edge.
The pair laws satisfy the paper's PairLaw m0 kappa hypotheses, with m0>0 and
kappa>0. For fixed_support_search_expectation/tail, every positive-probability
edge pattern is nonempty. Thus E is indeed the observed support and every
variable is active. The generated formula is well formed by construction.
Arbitrary fixed unit clauses are allowed.

For c0 = runtimeConstant m0 kappa, Lean proves

  E nodeCount <= 2^(ceil(n/2)+1) + 6*n*2^n*exp(-c0*n),

  P(nodeCount > 2^(ceil(n/2)+1) + 2*n*2^n*exp(-c0*n/2))
    <= 3*exp(-c0*n/2).

These results apply the actual candidate expectation and tail theorems;
they assume no concentration inequalities or candidate bounds.
retained_support_search_expectation/tail instantiate exactly the retained
pair law after conditioning on nonempty edge patterns. The nonemptiness
condition is derived from that law, not assumed again of its samples.
The earlier observed-support conditioning theorem supplies this product law;
its full integration over changing active sets is still pending below.

RuntimeRates proves c0>0 and absorbs the rounded prefix cost. With

  b(c) = exp(max((log 2)/2, log 2-c)),

it proves b(c)<2 for c>0, and hence

  E nodeCount <= (4+6*n)*b(c0)^n,
  P(nodeCount > (4+2*n)*b(c0/2)^n) <= 3*exp(-c0*n/2).

This maximum also accounts for the prefix cost when c is large; no hidden
assumption comparing c with log 2 is needed in the rate-conversion lemma.
It does not improve the paper's stated constants or claim a new input class.

Remaining obligations
----------------------
1. Reindex the active vertices of an arbitrary observed support in increasing
   original-label order, preserving its pair distribution, unit offsets,
   branching decisions, and initialized node count. General deterministic
   initialization and the fixed-support statistical bound are each checked;
   this probability/count transfer between them remains to be proved.
2. Combine observed-support conditioning with that transfer, including the
   empty support, compare r with the original n, and average over supports.
3. Complete the original input-model wrappers for the expected and
   high-probability runtime theorems. Polynomial arithmetic overhead and
   streamed space remain the ordinary paper implementation arguments,
   distinct from the recursive-call model and any compiler-level cost claim.

Count/projection accumulation keeps its preceding checked_core status.
The newly added lem:slice is separately checked in Counts.Slice, with direct
proofs of both its exact gain identity and its comparison inequality for
either polarity. It uses the existing clause-score count-adjustment identity.
Regular-graph and SBM membership/runtime proofs, hidden-community numerical
discovery and fallback integration, and the unproved appendix target are
outside this batch.

Audit and correspondence
------------------------
All new public theorems are included in Audit.lean. RuntimeExamples has
kernel-checked empty-support and immediate-rejection boundary cases, plus
executable evaluations on a Max-Cut pair and an early-pruning example.
The evaluations supplement the universal proofs; they are not certificates.
The full build, dependency audit, and source/manuscript fingerprints are in
result.json. Only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound are permitted.
The mathematical translation between paper and Lean still requires human
review; fingerprints and successful kernel checking do not establish it.
